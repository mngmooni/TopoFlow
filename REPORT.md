# 1. Introduction

Geometric path planning seeks collision-free paths connecting a start and a goal in environments containing obstacles. In such environments, multiple routes may exist that navigate around obstacles in qualitatively different ways. For fixed endpoints, these routes can belong to distinct homotopy classes, whose members cannot be continuously deformed into one another while remaining in free space. A generative planner should therefore be able to represent multiple route alternatives and, when desired, generate paths associated with a specified route class. However, specifying an intended route does not by itself ensure that a generated path satisfies the underlying geometric constraints.

Generative models offer a way to learn distributions over feasible path solutions rather than producing only a single deterministic route. Diffusion-based planning methods have demonstrated the potential of learned trajectory priors and cost-guided sampling, while Flow Matching provides a framework for learning continuous vector fields that transport a simple prior distribution toward a target data distribution (Carvalho et al., 2023; Lipman et al., 2023). Applying such models to obstacle-constrained path generation raises two interconnected questions: how can a generator distinguish between alternative topological routes, and how can its outputs be made geometrically feasible? Conditioning a generative model on a route descriptor may address the first question, but does not automatically provide a guarantee for the second. This motivates investigating representations in which route-specific geometric constraints are incorporated into the generative process itself.

Existing approaches address topological diversity and geometric feasibility through different mechanisms. Topology-guided planners explicitly explore alternative route classes, while recent generative methods incorporate topological information into trajectory generation. Separately, safety-aware diffusion and flow-based planners enforce geometric constraints through guidance, barrier functions, or optimization-based corrections. These advances demonstrate that neither topology-aware planning nor constrained generative sampling is new in isolation. However, they leave an important design question: how should topological route information and geometric feasibility be represented within a generative model, and what trade-offs arise when feasibility is enforced through the representation itself rather than through inference-time correction? In particular, preserving collision-free outputs is not sufficient to characterize a generative planner; it is also necessary to examine which route classes are representable, how well the learned model preserves within-class trajectory diversity, and what computational cost is required to generate feasible paths. These considerations motivate our investigation of topology-aware Flow Matching in class-specific feasible trajectory spaces.

To investigate these questions, we propose Topology-Aware Flow Matching (TDFM), a generative path planning framework that combines explicit topological route conditioning with class-specific feasible trajectory representations. We consider two-dimensional environments containing static obstacles and focus on a family of x-monotone paths. Each candidate route is described by a discrete code specifying whether the path passes above or below individual obstacles. Given an obstacle configuration and a route code, we construct a feasible chart that maps unconstrained latent coordinates to waypoint positions within geometrically admissible intervals. The chart is designed to preserve obstacle clearance along the resulting piecewise-linear path, rather than merely at individual waypoints. TDFM then learns a conditional Flow Matching model directly in these chart coordinates, allowing generated samples to be decoded into geometrically feasible paths without iterative collision correction. Under the stated chart-construction assumptions, geometric validity follows from the representation itself; corresponding topological validity requires additional conditions on the route representation and crossing cuts. This formulation enables us to study topology control, feasibility, and generative quality as related but distinguishable properties of a learned path distribution.

Our experiments reveal that explicit route conditioning and geometric feasibility are distinct requirements in generative path planning. Although route-conditioned Flow Matching models can closely follow their prescribed route descriptors, they may still produce trajectories that intersect obstacles. By contrast, both direct generation in feasible charts and chart-aware projection satisfy the tested geometric constraints, highlighting the role of the feasible representation rather than attributing validity to Flow Matching alone. Under matched evaluation conditions, TDFM preserves greater within-class trajectory diversity and requires less inference time than an adapted projection-based baseline, while the latter tends to generate shorter, smoother paths with greater obstacle clearance. Comparisons against class-matched sampling-planner references further indicate that TDFM captures much of the reference distribution's trajectory variation, although its generated paths tend to pass closer to obstacles. Beyond generation quality, our chart-based feasibility analysis identifies route classes that a sampling-based planner fails to discover despite the existence of chart-feasible paths. Together, these findings illustrate how topology-aware representations can support controlled generative path planning while exposing trade-offs among feasibility, diversity, computational efficiency, and class coverage.

We evaluate TDFM through a sequence of controlled experiments examining topological adherence, geometric validity, class coverage, generative diversity, and computational efficiency. Our results show that route-conditioned Flow Matching alone can maintain strong route adherence while still producing geometrically invalid trajectories. In contrast, chart-based generation and chart-aware projection both satisfy the tested geometric constraints, allowing us to isolate the additional effect of learning directly in chart coordinates. Compared with an adapted projection-based baseline operating on the same feasible charts, TDFM retains greater within-class diversity at lower inference cost, whereas projection produces shorter, smoother trajectories with larger obstacle clearance. Class-matched comparisons with a sampling-planner reference further reveal that TDFM better preserves the reference trajectory spread but tends to generate paths closer to obstacles.

The main contributions of this work are:

1. **Topology-conditioned generation in feasible charts.** We integrate explicit route-class representations with Flow Matching in a class-specific feasible trajectory space, and establish conditional geometric and topological validity statements for the decoded paths.

2. **A controlled analysis of feasibility, diversity, and computation.** We distinguish the effect of topology conditioning from that of feasibility-preserving representations and compare direct chart-space generation with chart-aware inference-time projection using matched trajectories and evaluation budgets.

3. **A class-coverage and feasibility audit.** We develop a chart-based procedure for analyzing the feasibility of candidate route classes, distinguishing certified infeasibility from failures of a sampling-based reference planner to discover feasible trajectories.

The present study focuses on static two-dimensional environments with circular and axis-aligned rectangular obstacles and an x-monotone trajectory family. Our findings characterize the capabilities and limitations of topology-aware generative planning within this setting, without claiming guarantees for arbitrary homotopy classes, robot dynamics, or continuous trajectory tracking.
